======= WILD CARDS AND EXTRAS

Your hero, unique allies, villains and monsters are collectively called ""wild" cards". These beings have a better chance at doing things, are a little tougher to put down and are generally more detailed than common guards, mminions, or lackeys -- collectively called "Extra"

Wild Cards can take 3 wounds before they're incapacitated

Wild cards roll a wild die (usually D6) along with their trait die when making trait rolls and take the highest of the two as their total

========= TRAIT ROLLS

To make a skill or attribute 'check' or 'roll' simply roll the die assigned to it. If the result is a 4 or better the action is successful. For example, if a character's strength is a d6 he rolls a six sided die. 4 or better wins.

Modifiers: Circumstances modify the die roll, such as shooting at something far away or inding a well-hidden clue. Some things, such as ranged attacks have standard modifiers. It's up to the GM to determine any modifiers for more subjective tasks, such as spotting an ambush or eavesdropping on a conversation through a door.

In general an east task such as finding tracks in the mud is amde a +2. A difficult task such as finding tracks by torchlight is made at -2. A very difficult task, such as finding tracks in a rainstorm, is made at -4.

Multiple dice: when a character rolls multiple dice, such as when firing a mchine-gun, check each die seperately.

======= THE WILD DIE

Extras roll a single die when making a trait roll. Wild cards roll an extra D6 and taer the highest of their trait die or the "wild die". Both of these dice can ACE.


ONE WILD DIE PER ACTION: When wild cards roll multiple dice for a single action, such as whenfiring a machine gun, they roll only one wild die.
The wild die may replace a trait die or be ignore. It never adds another action or attack to the roll.

Example: Gabe has the Grenzy Edge. He rolls an extra fighting die when making a melee attack along with a wild die. Only two of his dice can cause hits since the wild die can only replace a die, not add another attack.


========== RAISES

Sometimes it's important  to know just how successful a trait roll was. every 4 points over the TN is called a "rase". If your hero needs a 4 to shoot an opponent and rolls an 11, he hits whith one raise and would have 2 raises with a roll of 12. Figure raises AFTER adjusting for any modifiers.
A single raise always provides an additional effect of some sort, such as a bonus damage when attacking or a benefit determined by the GM for other rolls.


========= OPPOSED ROLLS

Sometimes rolls are "opposed" by an opponent. The attacker gets a Trait total, then the defender attempts to beat it.

The exact skills or attributes used in a contest are specificed in each circummstance, or the GM can decide what makes the most sense. If two characters try to pull something from each other's hands, for example, they make opposed strength rolls. Sneaking up on someone is a stealth roll opposed by Notice.

The acting character in an opposed roll always ges his Trait total first(including benefits) and must get at least a basic success(TN 4) or he fails. The defender rolls next and must meet or exceed the first character's result or the attacker wins.

The winner also uses his opponent's total for purposes of determining raise effects

============== CRITICAL FAILURES

A critical Failure occurs when a Wild Card rolls a 1 on both the skill die and the wild die of a trait roll. The attempt automatically fails and something bad happens -- a weapon is dropped or gets stuck and the attack hits a friend , the vehicle crashes, spells misfire, and so on.

Critical Failures cannot be rerolled, even with benefits.

Extras and critical failures: If an extra rlls a 1 on a trait check and its important to know if its a critical failure such as when casting a spell ,roll a d6. On a 1, it's a critical failure. Otherwise it's just a normal failure.

Mulriple Dice: Some abilities allow characters to roll multipe trait dice such as the frenzy edge or firing a weapon with a rate of fire higher than 1. A critical failure with multiplle dice occurs when more than haf the die results are a natual 1. If the character is a wild card, the wild die must be a 1 as well

Example: Gabe fires the auto cannon of a high tech thing. It has a rate of fire of 3 so he rolls threee shoting dice and one wild die. If 3 or more of the dice come up as 1 including the wild dice it's a critical failure.

============ UNSKILLED ATTEMPTS

If a character doesn't have a skill for an action he's attempting, he rolls a d4 for his skill die and a wild die if a wild card and subtracts 2 from the total.

============ REROLL

Some Edges or abilities allow a character to reroll traitss as long as they're not a critical failure. To do so, roll all the dice again and get a new total. You may keep any of the totals generated, so rerolls never make things worse unless you roll a critical failure. This ends all rerolls immediately and becomes the result of the attempt -- that's the risk you take in pushing the limits.

Multiple rerolls are allowed from different edges, benefits and other abilities

============= GROUP ROLLS

When you want to make a trait roll for a group of extras with similar traits, roll on trait die as usual along with a wild die and treat this as the groups result. This gives a decent average without maing rolls for every individual.

================= DAMAGE EFFECTS

Damage can result in 3 effects: Shaken, Wounds and Incapacitation


======== SHAKEN

Shaken characters are nicked, bruised, or otherwise rattled. They may only take free actions such as moving(including running). At the start of their turn shaken characters must attempt to recover from being shaekn by making a spirit roll. This is a free action.

Failure: The character reamins shaken. They can only perform free actions

Sucess: The character is no longer shaken and may act normally.

Spending benefits: A player may spend a benefit to remove Shaken at any time. Even if it isn't their turn.

========== WOUNDS

Every raise on the damage roll inflicts a WOUND. Extras are incapacitated it they take a sing wound and aren't RESILIENT. They're dead, injured or otherwise out of the fight.

Wild cards can take three wounds and still function more with cetain edges or abilities futher damage doesn't cause additional aounds buut incpacitates them instead

Wound penalites: each wound a character sufferes causes a -1 cumulative penalty to his pace(minimum of 1") and all trait rolls --- up to a maximum penality of -3

Critical failure: The character dies

Failure: Roll on the injury table. The injurt is pmermant and the character is bleeding out

Success: Roll on the injury table. The injury goes away when all the wounds are healds

Raise: Roll on the injury table. The injury goes away in 24 hours or when all wounds are healed

Characters cannot take actions and might be unconscious . The victim makes a vigor roll each day thereafter and is no longer incapacitated if successful. They may also heal wounds during this time

Bleeding out: the injured character is dying and must make a vigor roll at the start of his turn. Failure means he perishes. With success he survives but must roll again next turn or every minute if not in combat. With a raise he stabilizes and no fruther rolls are required.

Other characters may stop a victims bleeding by making a healing roll minus the patients wound penalties as usual. This is an action and if successful the patient is stabilized.

The healing power can also stabilize wound as can a natural healing roll by a being with regeneration of some sort


========= SOAK ROLLS

After rolling damage but before applying wunds, a character may spend a benefit to make a "soak" roll. This is a vigor check with each success and raise reducing the number of wounds suffered from the attack by one

If the characcter soas all of the wounds from an attack he removes hsi shaken condition too even from a previous source. Don't count the wound modifiers he's about to suffer when making this roll -- tat hasn't happened yet

Characters can't soak more than once per attack, but may spend benefits as usual to reroll the vigor check if they aren't satisfied with the results

=============== HEALING

The healing skill is used to remove wounds. Each attempt requires 10 minutes per wound level of the patient. Subtract 1 from healing rolls without a basic first aid kit or smilar supplies

A success removes one wound and a raise remove two. Failure means no wounds are removed. A critical failure increases the victims wound level by one.

Wound penalties: A healer subtracts his patients wound levels from his skill roll. A wounded character trying to heal his own or anothers injuries suffers from both effects. His wounds plus the wound penalty to the healing roll

THE GOLDEN HOUR: A character may only attempt to heal wounds on a patient once within the hour they were sustained. Failing the roll means the healer isn't able to treat those particular injuries. A different character may attempt a healing roll on the same patient, however

Once the wounds are over an hour old, only natural healing or the healing power using greater healing can heal wounds

Bleeding out: The healing skill can also be used to stabilize someone who's bleeding out. Each attempt is an action and if successful the victim is stabilized


incapacitation: Healing at least one wound on an incapacitated patient removes that state and reesotres consciousness if he was knocked out

================ NATURAL HEALING

Wounded characters make a vigor roll every five days. Success recovers one wound and a raise recovers two

A critical failure increases the victims wound level by one --either from infection, blood loss or aggravating the injuries. If this causes incapacitation don't use the usual rules for taking damage. Instead the victim lapses in and out of consciousness and makes a vigor roll every 12 hours. If the roll is failed, he expires. Success means he must roll again 12 hours later. With a raise he stabilizes and wakes. Allies a also attempt to stabilize the hero as explained under bleeding ut above.

Support: Characters with healing or survival skills can make support rolls to help allies when they make vigor rolls to heal, stabilize , or recover from any other ill effects. Support rolls do not subtract the victim's wounds from their total

================ SITUATIONAL RULES

The core rules are very simple. Roll a skill die and a wild die if a wild card. If the result is 4 or higher after all modifiers, you're successful.

========== AIM

Shooting rlolls assume a character is moving about, dodging attacks and eeping an eye on the chaos around them. If they focus their attention and aime, however, they can make a much more accurate shot


If the character spends her entire turn aiming a ranged weapon at a particular target and takes o other actions she can ignore up to 4 points of range, cover, called shot, scale, or speed penalties. Their attack must be used on their first action of their next turn or the bonus is lost

The shooter must be stationary to Aim. They can't walk, run, ride a horse or otherwise move under their own power. They culd aim from a vehicle traveling over or through a smooth surface, or one using the hold steady chase maneurver

======= AREA EFFECT ATTACKS

Grenades, spell effects, breath weapons and other attacks that cover a large area are "area effect attacks." The most common are small, medium and large blast templates and the cone template.

Cone templates are placed with the small and emanating from the attacker and fired with the shooting skills(or athletics for breath weapons and other natural attacs)

A basic success means the cone stats where the user intended and a raise causes additional damage as usual. Those beneath the template are hit or may evade if the attack allows it.

Failure meanse the attack didn't occur for some reason -- the creature failed to belch up noxious gas, the flamethrower malfunctioned, etc

To attack with a blast template the player places the templatte and rolls shooting or athletics(for throw weapons or breath attacks)
. If the attack is successful the blast lands where desired.

Any target even partially beneath either type of template is affected. If the effect causes damage, roll for each victim seperately. Attacs that hit with a raise cause bonus damage as usual.

======== Deviation

If an attack with a cone template fails, it goes over the targets' heads, hits the ground, or simply falters for some reason. 

If a blast template misses, it deviates 1d6" for thrown weapons and 2d6" for fired projectiles. Multiply by 2 if the attack made made at medium range, 3 if long and 4 for extreme.

Next troll a d12 and read it like a clock facing to determine the direction the missle deviates. A weapon can never deviate more than half the distance to the original target.

======== Cver and area effect attacks

Solid obstacles like treees or brick walls protect against area effect attacks if they're between the origin of the blast. Reduce the damage by the amount listed on the cover bonus table

=============== BOUND AND ENTANGLES

Grappling and powers like entangle cause characters to be bound and entangled. Here's what each of those states means and how to break free from them

Entangled: the victim can't move and is distracted as long as he remains so

Bound: The victim may not move, is distracted and vulnerable as long as he reamins bound and cannot make physical actions other than trying to break free

Breaking free is an action. With success, an Entangled hero is free of one particular entaglmenet or grappler. A bound character improves to entangled with a success; with a raise, he's free.

Breaking free from a foe: Use the grappling rule in reverse to break free -- the victim initiates an opposed athletics roll with whoever's holding him (either rmay roll strength at -2 instead to maintain or break free of the hold). If the victime's successful she improves her status one level, or two with a raise

=========== BREAKING THINGS

Occasionally a character may want to break a solid object, such as a weapon, lock or door. Use the wielder parry if held, or 2 if it's motionless. If a damage roll equals or exceeds the object;s hardness, it's broen, bent, shattered, or otherwise ruined

Most anything can be broken given enough time and effector so use this system only when attempting to break things in a hurry (such as during combat rounds)

==================== Evasion

Some attacks are slow or require the user to telegraph their deliver like flamethrowers or dragon's breath. Such attacks state they may be evaded. If an attack doesn't say it can be evaded it can't -- victims are simple hit if the attack is successful and take damage.

If an attack can be evaded and the chatacter is aware of it he makes an agility roll at -2. Those who are successful manage to avoid the attack and take no damage.

================== FATIGUE

Some hazards, stress, power or circumstances may cause fatifure instead of damage. This represents mental stress or minor but lingering injuries that make a person less effective. Fatigue stacks until the victim is incapacitated

Fatigued: The victime subtracts 1 from all trait rolls. If he takes another level of fatigue, he's exhausted.

Exhausted: the victim subtracts 2 from all trait rolls. If he takes anoter level of fatigue hes incapacitated

Incapacitated: The victim cannot perform actions and may be unconscious

============= RECOVERY

Unless otherwise specified by the source. Fatigue and Exhaustion improve one level per hour. Incapacitated characters are helppless and may be unconscious for 2D6 hours If treatment is possible(food, water, etc, depending on the source of fatigue) and a healing roll is made the character improves to exhausted

Mixed fatigue: If a hero suffers fatigue from different sources with different recovery times remove one level when the effect with the shortest duratin expires then another level when the longes expires

Hazards: Fatuge from hunger, thirst, etc. Sometimes has different recover conditions.
